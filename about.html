About PrivacyLens

Privacy policies are written to be legally complete, not to be read. PrivacyLens turns them into something you can actually scan.

How it works

You submit a privacy policy, either as a URL or as pasted text. The policy is analyzed and scored across five categories, then rendered as a label modeled on the nutrition labels found on packaged food. The goal is the same: take a dense technical document and surface the handful of facts a person needs to make a decision.

The five categories

Data collected
What personal information the service gathers, and how sensitive it is.
Third-party sharing
Who else receives your data, and whether you can opt out.
Retention
How long your data is kept, including after you delete your account.
User rights
What control you have — access, correction, deletion, portability.
Tracking
Cookies, pixels, fingerprinting, and cross-site following.

Reading the scores

Each category is scored from 1 to 5, where higher means more risk to you. A 1 means the practice is minimal or strongly user-protective; a 5 means it is extensive with little recourse. The overall score is the average across all five categories.

Research background

PrivacyLens grows out of undergraduate research on privacy measurement for motion sensor data conducted at North Carolina A&T State University. That work asks how much personal information can be recovered from sensor streams that appear innocuous. PrivacyLens applies the same measurement instinct to legal text: how much privacy risk does a policy actually carry, and can that risk be expressed in a form non-experts can act on?
